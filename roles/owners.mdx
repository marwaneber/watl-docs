## Orders

The screen the day actually runs on. It is the same list your managers use.

<Frame caption="Six live orders. Two are assigned to a driver; the rest are unassigned.">
  <img src="/images/owner-orders-desktop.png" alt="Owner Orders screen on a laptop with the full sidebar and a table of six orders showing status badges and assigned drivers." />
</Frame>

Filter with the tabs, move an order along with the **⋯** button, or click a row
to open it in full. **New Order** takes a phone order by hand.

### On a tablet

<Frame caption="At tablet width the sidebar stays open and the table scrolls within its frame.">
  <img src="/images/owner-orders-tablet.png" alt="Owner Orders on a tablet showing the sidebar and the order table with status badges visible." />
</Frame>

### On a phone

<Frame caption="The sidebar becomes a bar along the bottom with all six destinations.">
  <img src="/images/owner-orders-mobile.png" alt="Owner Orders on a phone with a bottom navigation bar and the order table." />
</Frame>

<Tip>
  On a phone or tablet, **swipe the order table sideways** to reach Status,
  Driver and Actions. Tapping a row opens the full order, which is usually
  quicker than scrolling the table.
</Tip>

## Menu

The first screen you land on. A restaurant can have more than one menu — a lunch
card and an evening card, say — and each holds its own items.

<Frame caption="One menu, holding six items.">
  <img src="/images/owner-menu-desktop.png" alt="Menu screen showing a menu card titled Carte with its description and an item count, plus an Add Menu Item button." />
</Frame>

- **Each card is a menu**, with its name, description and item count.
- **Click a card** to open it and work on the items inside.
- **Add Menu Item** is top right.

<Warning>
  Menu prices are live. Changing one here changes what customers see on your
  storefront immediately — there is no draft step.
</Warning>

## Staff

Everyone who can sign in, and what they are allowed to do.

<Frame caption="Four staff. The bottom row was invited but has not accepted yet.">
  <img src="/images/owner-staff-desktop.png" alt="Staff screen listing four people with email, a coloured role badge, position and an Active or Inactive status." />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Invite somebody">
    **Invite Staff**, then pick their role. They get a link to set their own
    password.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch for Inactive">
    Somebody stays **Inactive** until they open that link. It is a reminder to
    chase them, not an error.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change or remove access">
    The **⋯** button edits a person or switches their access off.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  Turning somebody off keeps their history — their past orders and checklists
  stay intact. Use it when a member of staff leaves.
</Info>

## Shifts

The weekly roster: who works which day, on which shift.

<Frame caption="The roster for the current week. Staff down the left, days across the top.">
  <img src="/images/owner-shifts-desktop.png" alt="Shifts screen with a weekly roster grid from Monday to Sunday, staff rows, a shift legend and counters for assignments, completed and pending." />
</Frame>

- **Move between weeks** with the arrows either side of the date range.
- **Three shift types** are colour-coded: Morning, Afternoon, Night.
- **The three counters** summarise the checklist work attached to the week, not the shifts themselves.
- **Create Worker** adds somebody to the rota.

<Note>
  A **worker** is a person on the rota. That is not the same thing as a **staff
  account** that can sign in. Somebody who only ever appears on the roster does
  not need a login.
</Note>

## Tasks

The checklists themselves. Write a template once — a closing routine, a cleaning
round — then assign it to people and shifts.

<Frame caption="Checklist templates, each with its item count.">
  <img src="/images/owner-tasks-desktop.png" alt="Task Templates screen listing several checklist templates with names, descriptions and checklist item counts." />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write a template">
    Give it a name and a list of things to check.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign it">
    **Select** opens a template so you can give it to somebody for a shift and a
    date range.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It reaches your manager">
    Assigned work shows up on the manager's Validator screen for sign-off.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Settings

Everything about how your restaurant presents itself and takes orders.

<Frame caption="The Branding tab, with your live storefront link on the right.">
  <img src="/images/owner-settings-desktop.png" alt="Settings screen on the Branding tab with restaurant name, description, logo and cover photo uploads, and an Online store panel showing the public store link." />
</Frame>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Branding" icon="palette">
    Restaurant name, slogan, logo, cover photo and an optional navbar logo. This
    is what customers see on your storefront.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Appearance" icon="wand-magic-sparkles">
    How your storefront looks to customers.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Location & Hours" icon="location-dot">
    Where you are and when you are open.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Order Timing" icon="stopwatch">
    How long you tell customers to expect to wait.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Staff Scheduling" icon="calendar">
    The rules behind the Shifts roster.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  The **Online store** panel holds your public link with buttons to copy it or
  open it. That link is what you give customers — put it in your bio, on your
  receipts and on the door.
</Tip>
